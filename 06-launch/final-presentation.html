Slide 5 · Strategy

RouteLogic keeps saying yes to every enterprise request, but the 95% of daily users who need speed, not scope, are quietly slipping away.

Value proposition

For fleet coordinators and dispatchers, the daily frontline users (the 95%), we restore speed and simplicity for daily tasks while preserving, not removing, the enterprise-grade features for those who still need them: a "hide, don't delete" approach. The urgency is user attrition already in motion: this is a response to something actively happening, not prevention.

If RouteLogic does nothing for 12 months

  • Risk: largest enterprise account already trialing a competitor, and its reference value with it.
  • Frontline disengagement: the 95% keep experiencing the tool as a burden.
  • Competitive gap: leaner competitors keep winning real-time coordination.
  • Reactive pressure: rushed, worse-quality changes or budget cuts.

Data-backed hypothesis · Optimizing the existing

Based on Coordinator interviews (UXR-02: "I reassign a route and the driver doesn't see it for ten to fifteen minutes"; "we keep a WhatsApp group as the real system"), the same dashboard lag from a night-shift Dispatcher (UXR-09), BUG-2044 (8 to 15 min reassignment delay), BUG-2072 (20 to 60 min dashboard lag), and Compliance Check averaging 14.6 min vs. a 3-min benchmark (CSAT 2.2), within a workflow where 69% of Coordinators don't reach Shift Handoff, we believe that solving dashboard reliability and Compliance Check efficiency for the Fleet Coordinator will make Coordinators trust the platform as their single source of truth and complete their daily workflow past Compliance, as measured by a 45–60% relative increase in completion past Compliance Check, from 31% to 45–50% by the end of the pilot. Guardrail: Route Assignment completion rate, currently 71%, must not drop. Go/no-go after one full pilot cycle.

Slide 6 · Research

The workaround is the competitor: texting dispatch and a paper manifest.

Current workaround (the status quo to beat)

  • Texts the dispatcher directly instead of the in-app "mark delivered" flow (3 taps, 3 screens).
  • Keeps a paper manifest as backup.
  • Re-locates buried core functions every shift: "Start Route" has no fixed position.

UXR-01: "To mark a stop delivered I tap through three screens. In the rain, at a doorstep, with a package in one hand. I've started just texting my dispatcher instead."

UXR-12: five of seven drivers keep a paper manifest "just in case the app dies". BUG-2055: no single-tap completion, top frontline complaint.

Competitive advantages

  • Real-time system of record: closes the data-integrity gap of texted updates and a stale dashboard.
  • Zero double-entry: eliminates the paper manifest.
  • Scales across the 95%: one product fix reaches every driver at once.

Future-state journey map · highlighted: moment of misery resolved

1 · Simple home screen

Sees "Start Route" and "Mark Delivered" front and center. Pain: core actions buried 2–3 levels deep (BUG-2079).

2 · One-tap completion

Completes the delivery in one motion, package still in hand. Pain: three taps across three screens (BUG-2055).

3 · Instant sync confirmation

On-screen confirmation the update reached dispatch. Pain: dashboard lag showing stale status (BUG-2072).

4 · Trust rebuilt

Stops texting the dispatcher; one single source of truth. Pain: off-platform texting (UXR-01).

PRD highlights · Must-haves

  • M1 Pre-fill from existing RouteLogic data.
  • M2 Confirm-all with exception editing.
  • M3 Empty-field highlighting.
  • M4 Same compliance record as today. M5 Preserve existing step tracking.
  • Won't: W1a AI suggestions for fields with no system data; W1b auto-submission until Compliance/Legal confirms validity.

What the PRD makes explicit: pre-filled data can be wrong. Any value updated more than 15 minutes ago is flagged and must be verified before "Confirm all", otherwise the feature repeats the trust problem behind the WhatsApp group (UXR-02).

Slide 8 · Validation · A/B experiment brief

Does pre-filled, one-click confirmation get Coordinators past Compliance?

Hypothesis

We believe the One-Click Compliance Checklist for Coordinators will make them trust the platform as their single source of truth and complete the daily workflow past Compliance, measured by a +14 change in workflow completion past Compliance Check within 4 weeks, while protecting Route Assignment completion (~71%).

Primary metricWorkflow completion past Compliance Check
BaselineCompliance Check 14.6 min vs. 3-min benchmark (CSAT 2.2); 69% don't reach Shift Handoff
MDE+14
GuardrailRoute Assignment completion (~71%); not below 66% at any point in 4 weeks
Design188 per arm · 50/50 · 4 weeks · p < 0.05, two-sided

Control (A)

Compliance Check logging takes 14.6 min today, nearly 5× the benchmark. Only 48% get past this step vs. 71% completing Route Assignment: a 23-point drop (CSAT 2.2, "Weak").

Variant (B)

Compliance Check opens pre-filled with data RouteLogic already holds; the Coordinator reviews and confirms in one action. FR1: 100% of fields with system data populated on load. FR2: one click submits when required fields are filled and no flag is open.

Held constant: Route Assignment flow, compliance record, tracking schema, no AI. Caveat: the variant bundles pre-fill, single-click confirmation and queue triage, so the effect can't be attributed to one mechanism.

Ship

Completion improves by ≥ +14 at p < 0.05 and Route Assignment never falls below 66%.

Iterate

Direction is positive but lift is below the MDE.

Kill

No improvement, or the primary metric moves negatively. Read date fixed at end of week 4; no peeking.

Slide 9 · Launch · GTM plan

M-tier targeted launch: engagement, not awareness.

GTM strategy

Goal: Engagement. Coordinators already use Compliance Check daily; the aim is adoption of the new flow over old habits (re-typing, WhatsApp cross-checks).

Audience: Coordinators at the 3 pilot accounts who complete at least one Compliance Check per week.

Tier: M · Targeted small reach, but silence risks contaminating the pilot read.

Channels (all owned)

Direct message to CoordinatorsLive or recorded walkthroughIn-app banner / tooltip

Timeline: internal CS brief and Support FAQ before pilot start; banner and walkthrough on Day 1; no push after launch.

Success metrics dashboard

≥80%

Enablement reach before first post-launch check (provisional)

≥50%

Adoption: "Confirm all" on first exposure (provisional)

> baseline

Support tickets: flag volume above pre-launch baseline

Likely post-launch decision: Iterate. 3 accounts and 4 weeks is too small a signal to double down.

Bad signal to watch

High reach, low adoption: understood but not trusted or not usable; the friction is the experience (or fear that pre-filled data is wrong). Low reach, low adoption: Coordinators didn't know the change happened, so the feature never got a fair test. Track the two metrics separately to tell them apart.

Slide 10 · Story

Numbers can invert our perceptions.

Friction points + aha moment

It was hard to read and analyse data. I also think about the fact that most of the time we don't have the data, or we don't know which metrics are best to analyse. Baselines are also hard to define. There is a lot of preparation, and in real life I think it is hard to follow all the steps.

Key takeaways / what's next

My biggest learning is how much numbers can invert our perceptions and qualitative data. Feedback is important, but it can also deviate from reality. Numbers are really explanatory.
