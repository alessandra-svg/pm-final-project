# Problem Hook & Value Proposition (Module 1)

## Responses
- **Chosen path, which scenario are you committing to? (StreamLine or RouteLogic, or your own):** RouteLogic Velocity
- **Strategic crisis, based on the brief, if the company does nothing for 12 months, what happens to its market position?:** If RouteLogic does nothing for 12 months: 
Risk: Largest enterprise account already trialing a competitor, likely to be lost, along with its reference value for future deals. 
Frontline disengagement: The 95% of daily users keep experiencing the tool as a burden, workarounds and internal pressure to switch grow. 
Competitive gap widens: Leaner competitors keep winning the real-time coordination use case RouteLogic was built for. 
Reactive pressure: Board concern turns into rushed, worse-quality changes (or budget cuts) instead of a deliberate strategy. 
Net effect: RouteLogic loses the operational use case that drives daily engagement, while its "complex but powerful" reputation makes new deals harder to win.
- **Moment of misery, what is the user likely doing outside the product (Google, spreadsheets, a competitor) because your tool isn’t serving them?:** The user is currently forced to navigate layers of enterprise-grade features and admin complexity just to complete simple, time-sensitive coordination tasks.
- **Problem hook, summarize the business risk and user pain into one urgent sentence.:** RouteLogic keeps saying yes to every enterprise request, but the 95% of daily users who need speed, not scope, are quietly slipping away, and they're the ones who actually keep the platform running.
- **Value proposition, based on the proposed initiative (Spotlight or Velocity), what is the new value and why is it urgent to launch now?:** For fleet coordinators and dispatchers, the daily, frontline users (the 95%), explicitly distinguished from the executive minority, we will restore speed and simplicity for daily tasks, while preserving (not removing) the enterprise-grade features for those who still need them, a "hide, don't delete" approach because frames urgency around user attrition already in motion isn't preventive, it's a response to something actively happening.
